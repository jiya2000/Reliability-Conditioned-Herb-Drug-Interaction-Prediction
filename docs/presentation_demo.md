# Live presentation demo

Launch from the repository root:

```sh
venv/bin/python -m streamlit run app/demo.py --server.address 127.0.0.1 --server.port 8501
```

Open http://127.0.0.1:8501. The separately trained local scaffold bundle must be
present under `artifacts/assay/scaffold/`. The app never trains on startup and
needs no internet connection during inference. Source links require internet.
To recreate a missing model, follow `docs/assay_workflow.md`.

## Three-minute walkthrough

1. Start with **St John's wort + Digoxin**. Show source-linked human findings
   and the observed study ratio. This is retrieved evidence, not an ML-predicted
   clinical outcome. Open **Live molecular model** to show the six-target
   hyperforin profile. Explain why the induction guard prevents using inhibition
   predictions to explain the observed human exposure decrease.
2. Enable **Demonstrate duplicate-citation handling**. The application repeats
   an existing record for demonstration. Unique publication count and evidence
   content remain unchanged. Publication IDs do not establish cohort independence.
3. Select **Echinacea + Digoxin**. Show the reported study-specific absence of
   an effect, with no universal safe verdict and no invented constituent inputs.
4. Select **Turmeric + Digoxin**. Show actual curcumin inference, prediction
   intervals, training-compound status and similarity. The demo catalog has no
   human trial for that pair; the system reports the gap. Optional exposure
   sensitivity uses an explicitly assumed concentration; its ratio is neither
   an AUC forecast nor a regulatory risk threshold.
5. Open **Explanation & audit**, then download the JSON report. Explain the
   trace from plant to compound, drug pathway, model, evidence and abstention.
6. Open **Model validation** to discuss the scaffold and publication holdouts,
   including weaker source-holdout performance.

## What is implemented

This is an integrated research screening system: trained molecular inference,
source-linked constituent/pathway mappings, a small abstract-checked human
evidence catalog, preparation-context checks, publication deduplication, an
induction/inhibition mismatch guard, and exportable provenance.

These components make a distinctive project implementation; they do not prove
scientific novelty or establish a validated clinical predictor. Clinical
abstention is a fixed safeguard, not learned clinical uncertainty. The human
findings are not generated by the molecular model. The optional exposure ratio
does not implement the full M12 clinical prediction framework.

## Evidence provenance

`data/demo/public_evidence.json` is separate from all historical datasets. It
contains four pair-specific records from three human publications. Metadata and
summaries were checked against PubMed abstracts on 30 October 2026; independent
dual review remains pending. No clinical consensus labels are created.

- [Digoxin parallel-group trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10546917/):
  the displayed ratio is a comparison of reported day-15 group means.
- [Cyclosporine trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12580993/): the ratio and
  interval are explicitly inverted from baseline/coadministration orientation.
- [Comparative digoxin study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18214850/): no
  numeric ratio is fabricated where none was extracted from the abstract.
- [Hyperforin mechanism](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10852961/),
  [GtoPdb hyperforin structure](https://www.guidetopharmacology.org/GRAC/LigandDisplayForward?ligandId=2764),
  [NCCIH turmeric constituent mapping](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/turmeric).

The JSON also records the cyclosporine prescribing-information pathway source
and the locally captured ChEMBL curcumin structure. The GtoPdb structure entry
notes tautomer ambiguity; the selected structure is recorded rather than
silently presented as a uniquely resolved form.
